Calendar

Shows your next events on the TV. It needs the calendar's link: in Google Calendar the "Secret address in iCal format", in iCloud the link of a public calendar, in Synology Calendar the published link.

    Smart home

      Screen

      Unless kept, ▼ steps from what is set here to everything, to only the photo, and back. ▲ opens what can be done with the photo on screen: mark it as a favourite, hide it, see where it was taken.

      Performance

      How much work the Pi does for the picture behind the clock, and so how much power it draws and how warm it gets. Menus are not slowed down by this: they, and the background's reaction to a button, are always drawn as fluidly as the screen allows.

      At 3840 × 2160 the Pi has four times as many pixels to fill as at 1920 × 1080, for everything it draws. With the second choice a 4K TV is sent 1920 × 1080 while the dashboard and its apps run, and enlarges the picture itself; small lettering is a little softer. When you leave for the desktop it gets its own size back. The TV goes dark for a moment at each change.

      The background stands still and photos stop moving until the Pi has cooled down.

      Night

      Between these times the TV shows only a dim clock on black. Any button brings the dashboard back for a minute. Times that follow the sun are those of the town under Weather, and need the weather set up.

      The picture darkens over the hour after sunset and is back to normal at sunrise.

      Only while the dashboard shows: not during an app, and not while music or the radio plays. The TV says so a minute before, and any button keeps it on. Needs a TV that can be switched over HDMI.

      Apps

      These appear in the app row on the TV, in this order.

        With Moonlight in the list above, paired with the PC: the games of the PC. One press wakes the PC and starts the game; open the PC once in Moonlight to fill or refresh the list. With RetroArch in the list: the games marked as favourites there, and the ones played last. The game played last comes first.

        One tile, Games, opens a page with the covers of all of them. Switched off, every game is a tile in the app row, after the apps.

        A program that needs its own command or options can be added in ~/.config/pidash/config.toml on the Pi. It then shows up here to reorder or remove.

        Other tiles in the app row

        Tiles of PiDash's own. Untick the ones you never use; ▲ on a tile in the app row hides it too. Settings always stays.

        Other devices on the TV

        A games console, a set-top box: each gets a tile in the app row that has the TV show it, so the dashboard is where everything on the TV starts. To come back, choose the Pi's input with the TV's own remote. Needs a TV that can be switched over HDMI.

          Radio

          Stations appear as tiles in the app row and play while the photos carry on. The remote's play button pauses.

                Videos

                Films and home videos, behind a "Videos" tile in the app row: folders to walk through with the remote, and a video plays full screen. A video that is broken off carries on where it stopped.

                The full path, like /home/pi/Videos. A USB drive or a share of the NAS that is mounted on the Pi counts. Several folders: put a semicolon between them.

                The shared folder on the NAS, like /video; several with a semicolon between them. Left empty, every shared folder the account may read is offered. The NAS account of the photos is used; it needs File Station's permission and read access to the folder.

                Videos are played by mpv, which the Pi may need first: sudo apt install mpv. On the remote: OK pauses, ◀ ▶ go back 10 seconds and forward 30, ▲ ▼ five minutes, 1 subtitles, 2 language, Back stops.

                Wi-Fi for visitors

                With a network entered here, the app row has a "Guest Wi-Fi" tile. It shows a code that visitors point their phone's camera at to join, without anyone spelling out a password.

                The password is shown on the TV to whoever opens that tile. A guest network of its own is the safest choice.

                Lock

                With a PIN, settings can be looked at but not changed without it.

                Updates

                Installed at night, between three and five, and never while an app is open.

                Backup

                One file with everything set up here, to move to another Pi or a new memory card. It contains the passwords and links stored on the Pi, so keep it to yourself.

                Files can only be saved and loaded on a computer or phone: open this TV's address followed by /settings there.
